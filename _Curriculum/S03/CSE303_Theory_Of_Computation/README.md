# CSE303 — Theory of Computation

## Description

Theory of Computation explores the fundamental limits of what computers can and cannot do. This course covers automata theory, formal languages, computability theory, and complexity theory — providing the mathematical foundation that separates engineers who merely code from those who truly understand computation.

## Learning Outcomes

- Construct and work with Deterministic and Non-deterministic Finite Automata (DFA/NFA)
- Design Regular Expressions and understand the equivalence with finite automata
- Master Context-Free Grammars, Pushdown Automata, and parsing concepts
- Distinguish decidable vs undecidable problems; understand the Halting Problem
- Classify computational problems by complexity: P, NP, NP-complete, NP-hard

## Syllabus Outline

- **Unit 1:** Mathematical Foundations — Sets, relations, functions, graphs, proof techniques
- **Unit 2:** Finite Automata — DFA, NFA, epsilon-NFA, regular operations
- **Unit 3:** Regular Languages — Regular expressions, pumping lemma, closure properties
- **Unit 4:** Context-Free Languages — CFGs, PDA, Chomsky normal form, pumping lemma for CFLs
- **Unit 5:** Computability — Turing machines, Church-Turing thesis, decidability, the Halting Problem
- **Unit 6:** Complexity Theory — Time complexity, P vs NP, NP-completeness, reduction techniques
