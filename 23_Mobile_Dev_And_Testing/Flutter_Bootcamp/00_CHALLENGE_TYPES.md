# Challenge Types — The Full Roster

Every `*b_*_Challenges.md` companion file in this bootcamp uses a mix of these formats. Different formats train different muscles — some make you think without writing a line, some drop you straight into someone else's broken code, some are just fast and a little competitive. Knowing the icon tells you what mode to switch into before you read the problem.

---

### 🎯 Predict & Verify
*(the original format — still most of the content)*

Read code, predict what happens, then run it and check. Trains reading comprehension of mechanism — you're proving you understand *why*, not just pattern-matching to a memorized answer.

---

### 🐛 Bug Hunt
Fully-written, plausible-looking code. No `TODO` markers, no hints about where the problem is. Something is wrong and it's on you to find it before you fix it — the way a real bug actually arrives. These are always based on genuinely common real-world mistakes, not contrived gotchas.

---

### 🎭 Two Truths and a Lie
Three confident-sounding claims about how something works. Two are true. One is a lie — often a very reasonable-sounding one. Find the lie and explain *why* it's wrong, not just which one it is. No code to write; pure "do you actually understand the mechanism or did you just memorize the happy path."

---

### ⚡ Speed Round
Five rapid-fire questions, answerable in under 2 minutes total. Gut-check your instincts — these test whether a fact is *automatic* for you yet, not whether you can reason it out slowly. Answers are one line each, no essay.

---

### 🔬 Diff Detective
Two near-identical code snippets, side by side. They look almost the same. They behave differently. Your job is to spot the *runtime* difference, not the textual one — the interesting bugs are never in what changed, they're in what that change actually does.

---

### 🧩 Code Jumble
A working function, correct and complete — but its lines have been shuffled into the wrong order. Reassemble it correctly. No new code to write, just structural reasoning: what has to happen before what, and why.

---

### 🏗️ Build It Blind
A plain-English spec. No starter code. No function signature. Not even a hint at what the return type should be. You design the API yourself before you write a line of the implementation — the way real feature work actually starts.

---

### ⚔️ Refactor Duel
Working code that gets the right answer the slow, ugly, or unidiomatic way. Your job: make it fast/idiomatic/clean *without changing what it returns*. There's a rubric, not just a single "correct" rewrite.

---

### 🕵️ Code Review
A snippet with reviewer comments already attached to specific lines — "this feels wrong, can you explain?" or "won't this break under X?" You have to actually understand what's being flagged and fix it, the way a real PR review conversation works.

---

### 👹 Boss Battle
One larger challenge at the end of a file's bonus round, combining several ideas from that week into a single build. Harder, optional, and meant to feel like a real payoff for everything above it — the "can I actually put this all together" test.

---

## How These Fit Into a File

Every companion file leads with the core **🎯 Predict & Verify** problems (the foundation — do these first). A **Bonus Round** at the end mixes in the other formats for variety and a change of pace. Nothing in the Bonus Round is prerequisite for later weeks — it's there because training the same muscle the same way every time gets stale, not because you need it to move on.
