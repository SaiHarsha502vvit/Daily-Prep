## 🧠 THE 6 POWER TECHNIQUES - APPLY DAILY!

> CHECKLIST: Did you use these today?

⬜ ACTIVE RECALL - Did you test yourself from memory?
• Close notes, write what you remember, then check

⬜ 45-15 BLOCKS - Did you use focused study blocks?
• 45 min deep work → 15 min break (NO PHONE!) → repeat

⬜ BLURTING - Did you dump knowledge on paper after learning?
• Write everything you remember, fill gaps in different color

⬜ 2-3-7 REVISION - Did you review previous days' topics?
• Day 2 review, Day 3 review, Day 7 review

⬜ FEYNMAN - Can you explain today's topic to a 10-year-old?
• If you can't simplify it, you don't understand it yet

⬜ QUESTION FIRST - Did you look at problems before theory?
• Trains your brain to study with PURPOSE

DAILY WORKFLOW:
───────────────
Morning: Look at problems FIRST → Study theory → Solve
Lunch: Blurt session - dump everything on paper
Evening: Revise today + previous days (2-3-7)
Night: Can you explain it simply? (Feynman test)

## Reality Check

**"Tracking habits is about discipline and willpower"** — Willpower is a finite, unreliable resource — good habit systems minimize the willpower REQUIRED through design ([12 psychology]'s systems-over-motivation): the environment designed for the behavior (the practice set up so starting is frictionless — the [13_DE dev-tools] make-the-right-thing-easy applied to habits), the habit stacked on an existing routine (the anchor that triggers it automatically), the small starting size (the two-minute version that beats the ambitious version you skip). "Discipline and willpower" is the exhausting model that fails when willpower runs low — the effective habit science is DESIGN (reduce friction, stack on anchors, start small, make it obvious and satisfying), which produces consistency without depending on the finite willpower that the discipline-model burns through and that fails exactly when you are tired or stressed.

**"Tracking everything gives the best insight"** — Over-tracking is friction that kills the habit of tracking itself, and most metrics do not drive action ([13_DE monitoring, 21_SDET Ch62]'s actionable-metrics, personal flavor): the elaborate tracking system abandoned because it was too much work (the [monitoring] dashboard-nobody-maintains, personal), the metrics tracked but never acted on (the [SRE] non-actionable alert). "Tracking everything gives insight" is the quantified-self trap — the effective tracking is minimal and actionable (the few metrics that actually change your behavior — the streak that motivates, the one leading indicator), because the elaborate system becomes a chore that gets abandoned, and the insight comes not from data volume but from tracking the few things that drive action, the same [monitoring, SRE] actionable-over-comprehensive applied to personal habits.

**"Breaking a streak ruins the progress"** — The all-or-nothing streak mentality is fragile and demoralizing — the resilient approach is never-miss-twice and quick recovery ([SRE]'s recovery-over-perfection, habit flavor): the perfectionist who breaks a streak and abandons the habit entirely (the one missed day that became a month off — the [12] catastrophizing), versus the one who misses a day and returns immediately (the recovery that preserves the habit). "Breaking a streak ruins progress" is the fragile all-or-nothing that turns one slip into total collapse — the durable model is aiming for consistency-not-perfection (the [SRE] MTTR-over-MTBF, personal — recover fast rather than never-fail), the never-miss-twice rule that tolerates the single slip while preventing the spiral, because the habit's value is in the long-run consistency (which survives occasional misses) not the unbroken streak (which one miss destroys, taking the demoralized person's whole habit with it).

**"Consistency means doing the same thing every day"** — Rigid sameness is brittle and ignores that life varies — sustainable consistency is FLEXIBLE (the minimum viable version on hard days, the adaptation to circumstances) ([SRE]'s sustainable-pace): the rigid routine that breaks the first time life interferes (the all-or-nothing that fails on the busy day), versus the flexible consistency (the full version when possible, the two-minute minimum when not — the habit that survives the variance of real life). "Same thing every day" is the rigidity that breaks on life's inevitable variation — the sustainable version has a scalable floor (the minimum that maintains the habit on hard days, keeping the identity and the streak alive) and a fuller version for good days, because the consistency that matters is showing-up-in-some-form (which flexibility preserves) not identical-execution (which real life defeats), the same [SRE] sustainable-and-resilient over rigid-and-brittle.

**"Habits form in 21 days"** — The 21-day myth is false — habit formation takes variable and usually longer time (the research shows a wide range, often months, depending on the behavior and person) ([12] psychology): the person who expects the habit locked in at 21 days and quits when it still feels effortful (the false-expectation that causes premature abandonment), the complex habit that takes far longer than a simple one. "21 days" is the pop-psychology myth that sets false expectations and causes people to quit when the habit has not "stuck" on schedule — the reality is variable-and-often-longer (frequently 2-3 months, more for hard behaviors), the effortfulness fades gradually not on a deadline, and knowing this prevents the premature-quit ("it should be automatic by now, I must be failing") that the false 21-day expectation produces, which is why patience-and-persistence past the mythical deadline is part of the actual habit science.
